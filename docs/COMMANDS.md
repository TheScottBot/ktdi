# Command reference

Every command in full: what it does, how to word things, and where its words come from. Grouped the same way as `/help`; the [README](../README.md) has the short version.

Commands work as slash commands (`/spray`) and as text commands with the prefix (`!spray` by default). Everything the bot remembers is per server unless `/settings shared_state` says otherwise.

<a id="rap-sheet"></a>
### 💦 Rap sheet

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `spray [@user]` | Posts a spray bottle GIF. Target someone to add to their rap sheet. |
| `allspray [exclude] [ping]` | Sprays everyone on the server at once (not bots, not you), except whoever you spare: `allspray exclude:@dave @pat`, or a whole role (`exclude:@Mods`). Each spray goes on that person's rap sheet as a normal spray (unless their insurance covers it) and counts for spray futures and wars, so it will start one. It's also tracked on its own, as an extra charge: everyone caught in one gets a line like "🚨 Was part of an unruly group 3 times" (or kettled, rounded up with the usual suspects...), and whoever started it "📢 Incited 2 riots". Nobody's pinged unless you add `ping:True` (typed: `!allspray @dave ping`). Needs the bot to see the server's members: see `SERVER_MEMBERS` in [SETUP](SETUP.md#allspray); until then it says it isn't set up. |
| React 💦 | Reacting 💦 to any message sprays whoever sent it and adds to their rap sheet. |
| `rapsheet [@user]` | Shows someone's sprays, times they've been bad, unruly groups they were caught in and riots they incited (`allspray`), expunges, bails, Don orders, sprays covered by insurance, bribes, committee spending, called predictions, bingo wins, 🍞 and spray futures (defaults to you). |
| `whospray` | Asks the server's Don (set with `/settings whospray_user`) who should be sprayed, with a random phrase. Only the Don is pinged. |
| `tdoi` | The Don Ordered It: sprays yourself, on the Don's orders. Counts as a spray, and as a separate "sprayed themselves on the Don's orders" count on your rap sheet. |
| `blame [reason]` | Blames a random person who's spoken recently in the channel. |
| `bribe <amount>` | Only the person last blamed in the channel can use it. Pays to put the blame back on whoever blamed them (who can then bribe it back). Bribe totals go on the rap sheet. |
| `expunge <amount>` | Pay the committee to remove one spray from your own rap sheet. Only works if you have a spray to remove. The payment counts as a bribe (toward your bribe total, the 👑 and the Champion Briber role), and your rap sheet shows how many you've had expunged. |
| `bail <@user> <amount>` | The counterpart to `expunge`: pay to remove one spray from **someone else's** rap sheet (if you think it was unfair). The payment counts as your bribe. Both rap sheets show it: "Bailed out of N sprays by others" and "Bailed others out N times". |
| `sprayfutures <@user> [stake] [hours]` | Bet that someone gets sprayed within `hours` (1 to 24, default 1), staking 1 to 10 sprays (default 1) per hour. On the line: stake × hours, plus the day's spray interest rate, rounded (e.g. 2 sprays × 3 hours at 15% = 7); the rate is fixed when you bet. The moment someone **other than you** sprays them (`spray`, a 💦 reaction, even `tdoi`), you win and that comes off your rap sheet (never below a clean record). If the time runs out, you lose and it goes on. Spraying them yourself doesn't count, you can't bet on yourself, and it's one open bet per person you're betting on. The result is announced where you bet, and bets survive a restart. |
| `sprayrate` | Today's spray interest rate: a whole number from 1 to 20%, picked each day at `SPRAY_RATE_TIME` in `.env` (default midnight, in `BOT_TIMEZONE`). One rate for the whole bot. |
| `insurance buy / status / complain` | Spray insurance, sold by the committee. `buy` costs $1,000 (paid to the committee, and counted as a bribe) for 2:00 to 9:59 of cover, announced publicly; buying again replaces your policy, no refund. While you're covered, sprays, `bad` and `badbonk` still happen (and sprays still pay out spray futures), but none of it goes on your rap sheet, which counts what insurance covered instead. `status [@user]` shows when a policy runs out, by the car clock. The catch: the committee runs two clocks. The **microwave** decides claims; the **car** clock is the countdown you're shown, and it's up to 20% out either way. Nobody announces when the real cover ends: you find out when a spray lands. `complain` gets you customer service. Neither clock is random: both come from your user ID and the UTC minute you buy (the sums are in `ktdi/lib/insurance.py`), so the same person buying at the same minute always gets the same cover. Working out your best minute, or someone else's worst, is the game. |
| `committee funds / spend / ledger / propose` | The committee's money. Every `bribe`, `expunge`, `bail` and `insurance buy` pays in (including ones from before it kept accounts). `funds` (or just `!committee`) shows the balance and recent spending; anyone can `spend <amount> <item>`, but not more than it has, and it goes on the `ledger` and their rap sheet; `propose` suggests something to argue about, and how many the funds would buy. Shared servers share one committee. |
| `bad [@user]` | Bad. Posts a random non-violent telling-off GIF (from `BAD_GIF_URLS` in `ktdi/features/fun.py`), optionally at someone, which goes on their rap sheet as "Has been bad N times" (separate from sprays), unless `insurance` covers them. |
| `badbonk [@user]` | The violent version of `bad`: a random GIF from `BADBONK_GIF_URLS`. Counts as being bad on the rap sheet, the same as `bad`. |

**Optional Champion Briber role:** if a server has a role named `Champion Briber`, the bot gives it to that server's biggest all-time briber after each bribe (and takes it off the previous holder). The bot needs **Manage Roles**, and its own role must sit above `Champion Briber` in the role list. Servers without the role, or where the bot lacks permission, are skipped. Change the name with `BRIBER_ROLE_NAME`.

<a id="fun"></a>
### 🎲 Fun

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `loot` | Declares you're looting the body (posts the perception check GIF). |
| `wars [number]` | The KTDI War Archives. 4 or more sprays (including 💦), `bad`s and `badbonk`s at other people in one channel within 5 minutes is a conflict: a **war** if 2 or more people were at it, or a **special military operation** if it was one person on a spree (until someone fires back, which makes it a war). Nobody's told while it's happening. Once nobody's done anything for 10 minutes, the committee suddenly publishes an official historical report in that channel: a gloriously disproportionate title drawn from what happened (wars: "The First Great Badening" for one fought mostly with `bad`, "The Incident at 21:47", "The Friday Night Massacre", "The Siege of Dave's Dignity", "The War of Twenty-Three Poor Decisions"; operations: "Sarah's Special Military Operation", "The Annexation of Dave", "The Unprovoked Incursion at 21:47", "The Sarah Doctrine"), when and how long, hostilities, belligerents, casualties (hits insurance didn't cover), the cause, the most aggressive (or commanding officer) and most persecuted, anyone who never fired back, the arsenal, the outcome and its historical significance. `wars` lists the archives (⚔️ wars, 🪖 operations); `wars 3` shows one in full. Insults (`vulgartongue insult`) count as hostilities too, but they're only words: a burst without at least 4 sprays and bads in it is a **diplomatic incident** (📨), its status set by how many insults flew, from "Diplomatic relations are tense" through "Ambassadors have been recalled" and "Diplomatic relations have broken down" to "A cold war has been declared". It's named after the insults that flew ("The Captain Queernabs Affair") and its report names the most offensive envoy, the most insulted and the words exchanged; insults flung during a real war show in its arsenal. Conflicts stay in their channel, even on servers that share state. Spraying yourself (`tdoi`) doesn't count. The archives only go back to when this was added: sprays weren't timestamped before. |
| `linux` | Asks our resident Linux hater (`LINUX_HATER_ID` in `.env`) a random question about how much they hate Linux. Only they are pinged. |
| `anime` | Asks our resident anime watcher (`ANIME_USER_ID` in `.env`) what they think of a random popular anime from [AniList](https://anilist.co), with a random question. Only they are pinged. If AniList is down it picks from a built-in list. |

### 📚 Words & wisdom

All the old books at once, then the shelf of words: dictionaries, idioms and slang, from books old enough to be out of copyright, and sometimes out of their minds. Each reply's title links to the exact spot in the book (and in a list of entries, each entry's name links to its own): Project Gutenberg pages scroll to the passage and highlight it (in most browsers), and archive.org scans open at the page with the word highlighted.

**The shelves.** The old books are on five shelves: 🦉 `nature`, 🧚 `folklore` and 🔮 `esoteric` (in Beasts & magic), 📚 `words` (here) and 🏠 `household` (in Hearth & herbs). A shelf with a thing asks every book on it at once, side by side (`/esoteric salt`). Pick a `book:` to ask just one (`/esoteric book:talisman thing:scarab`), with its entries suggested as you type. On its own, a shelf is a random entry from any of its books. Most books are only asked that way, which keeps Discord's command list short: they're below their shelf as `esoteric book:talisman`, and still work typed by their own names (`!talisman scarab`), which is how their examples are written. Typed, a shelf takes the book first: `!esoteric talisman scarab`. A few favourites (`pliny`, `omen`, `vulgartongue`, `deadword`, `beeton`, `cure`…) also have slash commands of their own, and are in the shelf's menu too. A book can be on more than one shelf, and a mostly-folklore book can put some entries on another (`omen`'s and `goblin`'s magic is esoteric too).

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `lore <thing>` | What all the old books have to say about something, side by side: `lore dragon` is Pliny's, Browne's and Topsell's dragons, the Book of Secrets' dragon stone, Miller's dream of one, Brewer's idioms and whatever else turns up, one entry from each with where it mentions the thing and a link to the page, then the command that has the rest (`/pliny dragon`, `/esoteric book:talisman thing:dragon`). Books with an entry for the thing itself come first, then ones that mention it; old spelling is found too ("love" finds "loue"). Typed, `!oldbooks` works too. It searches every old book that declares itself (see Adding an old book, below); the almanac, weather sayings and a few others go by date or chance, so they only turn up in Old Wisdom of the Day. |
| `wisdom` / `wisdom setup here [time]` / `wisdom setup show` / `wisdom setup stop` | `wisdom` is a random piece of old wisdom from any of the old books (Pliny, Topsell, the Book of Secrets, Mrs Beeton, Bierce, Grose, Miller, the almanac, Culpeper, the weather sayings and the rest), with the command that has more from the same book; each book's command is as likely as any other, however big the book. **Old Wisdom of the Day:** someone with Manage Server runs `wisdom setup here` in a channel, and one is posted there every day at 9am where the bot is (or the time given: `wisdom setup here 7:30am`), from a different book each day. `wisdom setup show` says where and when; `wisdom setup stop` stops it. One channel per server: set it up in another and it moves. It's stored in the database, not `.env`, so nothing needs a restart. A day missed by hours (the bot was down) is skipped rather than posted late. |
| `bread` / `bread scores` / `bread setup here [time]` / `bread setup show` / `bread setup stop` | **How well do you 🍞?** (Somerset for "how well do you be read": how well-read are you?) A question a day from the old books: what a lost word or a bit of 1811 slang meant, how Bierce defined something, what a flower said in the Victorian language of flowers, or what you'd have done at a Victorian dinner, on Plough Monday in 1826 or after spilling the salt. Four answers, one right: the wrong ones are other entries from the same book, so they sound right too. Press a button to answer, whenever you like: only you see your answer, and there's no changing it. **The daily bread:** someone with Manage Server runs `bread setup here` in a channel (9am where the bot is, or the time given), and the first question goes up straight away; each day after, one post gives yesterday's answer (who got it right, how many picked each, the book's own words and a link, and the week's top bakers) and then the new question. `bread` on its own shows you today's question (privately, to answer if the post's scrolled away) and yesterday's answer. `bread scores` is the table, with streaks and everyone's loaf (Crumb, Crust at 5 right, Bap at 15, Bloomer at 30, Cottage loaf at 60, Master baker at 100). Everyone who gets the day's question right gets a 🍞 on their rap sheet (🍞🍞🍞, then a count past a dozen). A server gets every question before any comes round again. About 2,700 questions: built from the books' entries and every one read, with the ones that broke the house rules or weren't fair left out (`ktdi/lib/bread_curation.json`), plus scenarios written by hand from a passage and checked against it. To rebuild after a book changes, run `python -m ktdi.tools.bread_build`. |
| `words [book] [thing]` | The 📚 words shelf: Grose's slang, Mackay's lost words, Bierce's *Devil's Dictionary* and Brewer's phrases. `words drunk` asks them all, an entry from each, like `lore`; its books are `devils` and `phrase` below, and `vulgartongue` and `deadword`, which have commands of their own too. |
| `words book:devils [word]` (typed: `!devils`) | Ambrose Bierce's cynical definition of a word, from *The Devil's Dictionary* (1911, public domain, via Project Gutenberg): **bore** (a person who talks when you wish him to listen), **lawyer** (one skilled in circumvention of the law), **reason** (to weigh probabilities in the scales of desire)... with his verse and asides, and the made-up poets he credits them to. On its own it's a random entry; `devils <word>` looks one up (typos are fine, and it autocompletes on the shelf), or, for a word he didn't define, lists the entries that use it (`devils money`). Very long entries are cut short, with the rest a click away. Typed, `!devil` and `!devilsdictionary` work too. Bierce is cruel to everyone, which is the point, but entries that are racist, sexist or mock disability beyond that were read and taken out; only what was kept is recorded (an allowlist, `ktdi/lib/devils_curation.json`), and a test checks the rest stays out using fingerprints. To add an entry, read it in the book, add its headword to that file and run `python -m ktdi.tools.devils_build`. |
| `words book:phrase [idiom]` (typed: `!phrase`) | Where an idiom comes from, according to the Rev. E. Cobham Brewer's *Dictionary of Phrase and Fable* (1898, public domain, via archive.org), who was very sure of his etymologies: **mad as a hatter**, **to let the cat out of the bag**, **to rain cats and dogs**... Word it however you like (`phrase raining cats and dogs` finds "To rain cats and dogs"; typos are fine and it autocompletes on the shelf); for a word that isn't an idiom it lists the idioms that mention it (`phrase secret`). On its own it's a random one. Typed, `!idiom` and `!brewer` work too. About 1,800 idioms were hand-picked from the scan's OCR into `ktdi/lib/phrases.json` and mended against the page (Brewer's thousands of gods, saints and poets are left out, and so is anything that punches down), each linked to its page in the scan. |
| `vulgartongue lookup [word]` / `vulgartongue random` / `vulgartongue insult <@user>` | Slang from Francis Grose's *Dictionary of the Vulgar Tongue* (first published 1785; this is the 1811 edition, enlarged after his death, public domain, via Project Gutenberg). `vulgartongue insult @dave` insults Dave with a random 1811 insult, pinging only him (enough of them in a channel is a diplomatic incident: see `wars`) ("@Scott has insulted @Dave, in the words of 1811: **Captain Queernabs!** A shabby ill-dressed fellow"; also **Admiral of the Narrow Seas**, **Grinagog**, **Knight of the Trencher**...). `vulgartongue lookup <word>` looks a word up (typos and word order are fine, and the slash version autocompletes), or, for a plain word, finds the slang for it: `drunk` gives Grose's entry plus a dozen other ways to say it, `coward` lists the 1811 words for a coward. Handy for NPC dialogue. `vulgartongue random` (or `lookup` with no word) is a random phrase, never an insult. Typed, `!vt drunk` (or `!vulgartongue`, or `!slang`) looks it up and `!vt` on its own is a random phrase; in the slash menu, `/vt` finds it. Every slur, and every entry that's sexist, racist, xenophobic, homophobic or transphobic, about sex, or mocking disability or bodies, has been taken out, by word list and then entry by entry. Only what was kept is recorded (an allowlist, `ktdi/lib/slang_curation.json`, which also picks the insults), so nothing that was taken out is in the repo; a test checks it stays out using fingerprints, not the words. To add an entry, read it in the book, add its headword to that file and run `python -m ktdi.tools.slang_build`. |
| `deadword [word]` | A word English has stopped using, from Charles Mackay's *Lost Beauties of the English Language* (1874, public domain, via archive.org), a book written to bring them back: **bever** (a drink between meals), **bell-penny** (money laid up to pay for your own funeral)... with Mackay's definition, a line showing it in use, and an epitaph. On its own it's a random one; `deadword <word>` looks one up (typos are fine, and the slash version autocompletes), or, for a plain word, finds the dead words that meant it (`deadword drink`). The words were hand-picked into `ktdi/lib/deadwords.json`: only ones that are really dead now (Mackay also championed words that recovered), with nothing that demeans anyone. |

<a id="beasts-and-magic"></a>
### 🐉 Beasts & magic

The shelves of natural history, folklore and magic: the old natural histories, which believed everything, the books of magic, which promised everything, and a Victorian cabinet of curiosities, which collected everything. From the same kind of books, linked the same way.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `nature [book] [thing]` | The 🦉 natural history shelf: `bestiary`, `pseudodoxia` and `wonder` below, and Pliny and Culpeper's plants (`pliny` and `plant` have commands of their own too). `nature elephants` asks them all, an entry from each, like `lore`. |
| `nature book:bestiary [beast]` (typed: `!bestiary`) | Edward Topsell's bestiary, *The Historie of Foure-footed Beastes* (1607) and *The Historie of Serpents* (1608), in which a Jacobean clergyman gathered everything ever said about every animal, believed nearly all of it, and put the unicorn, the gorgon, the lamia and the winged dragon in among the badgers and the cats: cats' breath that rots your lungs, panthers caught by getting them drunk, the cockatrice that is almost dead when it hears a cock crow. Give a beast (`bestiary unicorn`, `bestiary toad`) for a passage about it, by a modern name (which autocompletes) or anything that mentions it; ask again for another. On its own: any passage. It's in his own spelling ("Vnicorne", "haue"), from the Text Creation Partnership's hand-keyed transcriptions (EEBO-TCP, public domain), and links to its page in the scan of the printed book on archive.org. Typed, `!topsell` works too. About 470 paragraphs on about 150 beasts, picked by hand and then checked again: nothing racist about real peoples, nothing sexual or cruel, and none of his many remedies. Only their keys are recorded (`ktdi/lib/bestiary_curation.json`). To add one, read it in the book, add its paragraph number to that file and run `python -m ktdi.tools.bestiary_build`. |
| `nature book:pseudodoxia [topic]` (typed: `!pseudodoxia`) | Sir Thomas Browne's *Pseudodoxia Epidemica*, or "Vulgar Errors" (1646, public domain, via Project Gutenberg): a whole book of what everyone wrongly believed (that a badger's legs are shorter on one side, that elephants have no joints, that a kingfisher hung by its beak shows which way the wind blows), each one stated and then solemnly taken apart. Give a topic (`pseudodoxia badgers`, `pseudodoxia unicorns`) for a chapter about it, by his own chapter titles (which autocomplete) or anything that mentions it; ask again for another. On its own: any chapter. Typed, `!vulgarerrors` and `!browne` work too. About 60 chapters, read by hand and picked for the creatures, marvels and beliefs (not the chapters on Jews, on "the blackness of Negroes" or on Gypsies, and not the dense theology); only their keys are recorded (`ktdi/lib/pseudodoxia_curation.json`). To add one, read it in the book, add its key (`3.5`: book, chapter) to that file and run `python -m ktdi.tools.pseudodoxia_build`. |
| `nature book:wonder [topic]` (typed: `!wonder`) | A curiosity from *Ten Thousand Wonderful Things* (ed. Edmund Fillingham King, first published about 1860; this is the 1894 edition, public domain, via Project Gutenberg), a Victorian cabinet of curiosities: remarkable objects, old customs, eccentrics, inventions, animals and extraordinary claims, with the book's engraving when it has one. On its own it's a random one, for stumbling on something odd; give a topic (`wonder giants`, `wonder clocks`) for one about it (the titles autocomplete), and ask again for another. Each is numbered in book order ("Wonder No. 312": the book doesn't number them) and links to its place in the book. They're the book's claims as it made them, not checked facts, and the footer says so. Typed, `!wonders` and `!curiosity` work too. The articles were read by hand and the best kept, leaving out the dull and what's Victorian at its worst (the racism, gloating over executions and torture, cruelty to animals); only their titles are recorded (`ktdi/lib/wonder_curation.json`). To add one, read it in the book, add its title to that file and run `python -m ktdi.tools.wonder_build`. |
| `pliny [topic]` | Pliny the Elder's *Natural History* (AD 77, in Bostock and Riley's 1855-57 translation, public domain, via Project Gutenberg), in which a Roman admiral wrote down everything anyone ever told him: elephants that worship the moon and write Greek, the basilisk that kills with a look, dragons, the phoenix, showers of milk and baked tiles, mares impregnated by the wind. Give a topic (`pliny elephants`, `pliny amber`) for a chapter about it, by his own chapter titles (which autocomplete) or anything that mentions it; ask again for another. On its own: any chapter. Typed, `!naturalhistory` works too. About 440 chapters from the books on the world, man, animals, remedies from animals, metals and stones, read by hand and picked for being gloriously wrong (nothing racist about real peoples, and no remedy anyone might try); only their keys are recorded (`ktdi/lib/pliny_curation.json`). To add one, read it in the book, add its key (`VIII.3`: book, chapter) to that file and run `python -m ktdi.tools.pliny_build`. |
| `folklore [book] [thing]` | The 🧚 folklore shelf: `goblin` and `flowermeaning` below, the wonders again, and the everyday superstitions (`omen`, which has a command of its own too). The almanac and the weather sayings are on it as well, by their own commands, and turn up when it's asked on its own. `folklore salt` asks them all, an entry from each. |
| `folklore book:goblin [what]` (typed: `!goblin`) | A tale from Wirt Sikes's *British Goblins* (1880, public domain, via Project Gutenberg), the folklore of Wales as the American consul in Cardiff gathered it, from the people and the old books: the Tylwyth Teg (the fair folk), the Pwca, mine goblins that knock, lake maidens, corpse candles, spectral hounds, ghosts, old wedding and funeral customs, and legends of bells, wells, stones and dragons, with T. H. Thomas's illustrations where there are any. Give a creature, place or custom (`goblin pwca`, `goblin corpse candle`) for a tale about it (the titles autocomplete; Welsh and English names both work, and typos are fine); ask again for another. On its own: any tale. Typed, `!goblins` and `!fairies` work too. The book's short numbered sections were read by hand and the best kept, each given a title (mostly from its chapter's own list of topics) and the names it might be asked for by; only those are recorded (`ktdi/lib/goblin_curation.json`). To add one, read it in the book, add its key (`1.2.4`: book, chapter, section) and a title to that file and run `python -m ktdi.tools.goblin_build`. |
| `folklore book:flowermeaning [flower or meaning]` (typed: `!flowermeaning`) | What a flower meant in the Victorian language of flowers, from Henrietta Dumont's *The Language of Flowers* (1851, public domain, via Project Gutenberg), when people sent messages in bouquets: a pansy is "Think of me", basil "Hate", an asphodel "My regrets follow you to the grave". Give a flower (`flowermeaning pansy`) for its meaning, with the book's prose and verse on it if it has a chapter, and its coloured plate if it's in one ("Your self-love and stupidity excite my pity"); the names autocomplete. Or give a meaning (`flowermeaning love`, `flowermeaning grief`) for a flower that says it, with the others that do. On its own: any flower. They're Dumont's meanings: other books of the day gave others, and the footer says so. Typed, `!flower` and `!floriography` work too. About 270 flowers, read by hand: any chapter that breaks the house rules (verse running down women, plants recommended as cures, sneers at peoples) is left out and the flower kept with just its meaning (`ktdi/lib/flower_curation.json`). To change one, edit that file and run `python -m ktdi.tools.flower_build`. |
| `omen [what]` | An everyday superstition and where it came from, from Robert Means Lawrence's *The Magic of the Horse-shoe, with Other Folk-Lore Notes* (1898, public domain, via Project Gutenberg): why a horseshoe over the door is lucky (and which way up), spilling salt, blessing a sneeze, unlucky Fridays and lucky days, rats summoned to court, charms against animals, the luck of odd numbers and the ill luck of thirteen. Give one (`omen salt`, `omen thirteen`) for a passage on it (the titles autocomplete; typos are fine); ask again for another. On its own: any omen. Typed, `!omens` and `!superstition` work too. The book is seven long essays, so what's kept is a paragraph or a short run of them, read by hand and each given a title for what it's about; only those are recorded (`ktdi/lib/omen_curation.json`). To add one, read it in the book, add its key (`3.5.2`: essay, subsection, paragraph; `"to"` for a run) and a title to that file and run `python -m ktdi.tools.omen_build`. |
| `esoteric [book] [thing]` | The 🔮 esoteric shelf: magic. `secrets`, `dream`, `gemlore`, `talisman` and `magicsquare` below, and Culpeper's astrological cures (`cure`, which has a command of its own too); and the charms and spells in the folklore books, so `esoteric salt` is salt against witches and salt as a magical substance, not salt at dinner. `hammeredofwitches` is on it as well, by its own command. |
| `esoteric book:secrets [what]` (typed: `!secrets`) | A secret from *The Boke of Secretes of Albertus Magnus* (English, 1560, public domain): the magic of herbs, stones and beasts, and the marvels of the world. Not really by Albertus (a medieval book that borrowed his name), and none of it works, which is the charm: a lodestone under your wife's head to learn if she's faithful, marigold and a wolf's tooth so nobody can speak against you, a lamp that gives everyone in the room a dog's face, a lamp that makes whoever holds it fart. Give what you're after (`secrets enemies`, `secrets invisible`, `secrets fire`) or what it's made of (`secrets amethyst`, `secrets owl`; these autocomplete), in modern spelling: the book's own ("wyfe", "loue") is found either way. Ask again for another. On its own: any secret. From the Text Creation Partnership's hand-keyed transcription (EEBO-TCP), linked to its page in the scan of the printed book. | `rpg search <query>` / `rpg download <number or name>` | Search the RPG rulebooks in a Dropbox folder (words from the name or folder, any order: `5e player`) and get a private download link (optional; see below). |
| `settings show` / `settings shared_state <true/false>` / `settings whospray_user [@user]` | Per-server settings, stored in the database. **Shared state** (on by default, needs Manage Server to change) pools quotes, sprays, bribes and campaigns with every other server that has it on; off keeps them local to that server, and switching loses nothing. **whospray_user** chooses the Don, who `/whospray` asks and `/tdoi` obeys (leave it empty to clear); only the user IDs in `WHOSPRAY_ADMIN_IDS` in `.env` can change it, not even server admins. |
| `help [command or section]` | Where to start (`/spray`, `/rapsheet`, `/bad`, `/quote random`, `/nowplaying`) and the sections, a line each, with a menu to open any section: it opens just for you, so anyone can use the menu. `/help old oddities` (or any section's name, loosely) opens a section; `/help abm` (or any command) gives the details, like every unit `/abm` understands. Sections list each command once; a command with subcommands (`/quote …`) lists them on its own page. The slash version autocompletes sections, commands and subcommands as you type, by any of their names (`np` finds `nowplaying`). |
